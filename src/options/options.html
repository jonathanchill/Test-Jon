Mail Lens

Flags Gmail messages as Spam, AI-written or Human, in the message list and inside the open message.

Claude API key optional

Mail Lens runs fully offline by default — no account, no key, no cost, and nothing leaves your machine. A key only sharpens one call: telling human writing from AI writing in a message you open. Spam and automated mail are judged offline either way.

If you want it: keys come from the Claude Console, which is billed separately from any Claude.ai Pro or Team subscription.

The key is kept in this browser profile's extension storage. Anyone with access to this Chrome profile can read it. Use a key scoped to a workspace you can revoke.

Model

What gets checked

In offline mode nothing is sent anywhere and there is no cost or limit. In Claude mode, rows are batched eight per call, only rows you scroll past are checked, and every verdict is cached for 30 days.

Today

How much to trust it

SPAM and AUTO are the reliable badges. Junk has blunt tells, and automated mail announces itself in the sender address and footer — offline scoring handles both well.

HUMAN vs AI is the weak one, and offline it is weaker still. No detector can prove text was machine-written. Fluent writing by a careful human is the common false positive, a light human edit defeats detection entirely, and from a one-line preview there is usually nothing to go on — which is why you will see ? a lot, and should. Read the percentage as the strength of a hunch, not a measurement, and treat a badge on someone's mail as a prompt to look closer rather than a verdict about them.

Mail Lens reads what Gmail has already drawn on screen. On the default offline setting that text never leaves your browser. Only if you switch a mode to Claude is the sender, subject and message text of the mail you look at sent to the Anthropic API — and nowhere else, ever.
